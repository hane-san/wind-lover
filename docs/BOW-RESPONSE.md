# Bow response, deliberate selection and readable pressure — 25 October 2026

This extends [the fixed grip/interceptor design](GRIP-AND-SWEEPER.md). The owner wants the bow action refined thoroughly for natural control and combat rhythm. The supporting hand, approved composite bow, substantial 110–170px stroke, .8 commitment, .24s recovery, 420m range, one-arrow kills, independent reins and staged destruction remain the foundation.

## What was corrected

1. **Small, slow steering was being swallowed.** Before steering, the long-pull settling code moved its reference to the whole hand position whenever the angle was small. Repeated 1px sideways steps could therefore move 40px without ever steering. Settling now projects only along the original draw axis; perpendicular movement accumulates. This preserves both long diagonal extensions and deliberate slow corrections.
2. **Two filters delayed the same aim.** The old 18/s cursor filter fed a second 16/s graphic-angle filter. One 32/s aim filter now feeds the graphic angle directly. Scale still eases at 16/s around the fixed grip. Moving assisted targets update the aim goal, rather than bypassing the filter; entering manual steering starts from the current displayed aim. A little hand movement eases through the onset instead of abruptly applying a large turn at 20px.
3. **A release could choose an unseen neighbour.** `releaseTarget` only validates the identity last shown in the brackets. A dead, hidden, reserved, rearward or out-of-range target yields a free arrow along the last visible aim ray. It never acquires another foe on release. New targets continue to require the existing 65ms deliberate-steering confirmation.
4. **Selection was sometimes too generous in the wrong direction.** A valid enemy within 22px of the original direct touch is selected by nearest screen position before the broad assistance ranking. Once steering, acquisition is restricted to a .24rad cone and retention to .34rad, with a 12px floor. The initial generous touch assistance remains; manual aiming distinguishes adjacent foes more clearly.
5. **The old absolute sky-angle clamp could jerk a low or near-side aim.** Counter-rotation now stays relative to the captured direction. Viewport fitting, ahead/visibility checks and the angular control reference bound the result instead of forcing every shot into the same upper-screen sector.
6. **A stationary prepared follow-up had no readiness cue.** The same readiness helper now runs on draw movement and the simulation update. It sounds once when recovery finishes even if the finger is still, resets on letting down, and stays silent while paused/dead. Recovery is unchanged and releasing too early still cannot silently queue a later shot.

The 8px steering threshold and eased .025rad angular slack filter small jitter. Extending a straight draw never steers; extending an already steered ray preserves its angle. Full commitment, cancellation and fast physical release remain separate from visual smoothing.

## Pressure has a readable sequence

The interceptor may begin its .95s tell only between launcher cues, after scheduled emissions have left and with more than .22s until the next cue. While it is telling/diving, another launcher cue waits; after it ends, the remaining .22s plus the normal .62s missile tell gives a follow-up opportunity. Missiles already in flight remain real and dodgeable. Destroying the wing ends its pressure immediately. Salvo limits, five/six patrol slots, enemy health, projectile paths, frozen aim, contact cores and scenic rest are unchanged.

This produces a concrete choice: keep dodging existing fire and interrupt the committed wing, or evade its frozen line and shoot a launcher next. It reduces simultaneous new demands rather than adding targets, faster missiles or hidden invulnerability.
